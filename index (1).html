The Problem

America's small businesses are being
left behind — not by technology,
but by communication.

AI is reshaping the American economy. Large corporations are embedding it into every layer of their operations, widening a productivity gap that already leaves small businesses operating at less than half their potential.

The barrier is not technical. Federal data shows that most small business owners have simply never received a clear, accessible explanation of what AI could do for their specific operation.

The gap is one of communication.

Framework Component 02 · Plain-Language AI Glossary

Every term, in plain English.

No jargon, and no assumed technical background. Search or browse — every entry below is written for a business owner, not an engineer.

Artificial Intelligence (AI)

Software that can perform tasks — recognizing patterns, answering questions, making recommendations — that normally require human judgment.

Why it matters"AI" covers many different tools. The specific tool matters far more than the label.

Machine Learning

A way of building AI where a system improves at a task by learning from examples, rather than being programmed with fixed rules.

Why it mattersMost everyday AI you already use — spam filters, recommendation engines — works this way.

Generative AI

AI that creates new content — text, images, or audio — from a prompt, instead of just analyzing existing data.

Why it mattersAI writing assistants and image generators fall into this category.

Large Language Model (LLM)

The technology behind AI chat tools, trained on huge amounts of text so it can hold conversations, answer questions, and draft writing.

Why it mattersThis is what powers most products marketed to you as an "AI assistant."

Chatbot

A program that holds a text or voice conversation with your customers — often to answer common questions or take orders.

Why it mattersUsually the lowest-cost, fastest entry point into AI for a small business.

Prompt

The instruction or question you type to an AI tool to get a response.

Why it mattersBetter prompts get better results — this is a skill you build, not a technical setting.

Automation

Using software to handle a repetitive task — scheduling, reminders, data entry — without a person doing it manually each time.

Why it mattersAutomation existed before AI. AI just makes it more flexible and less brittle.

AI Agent

An AI tool that can take multiple steps toward a goal on its own — like booking an appointment or drafting and sending a follow-up — rather than answering one question.

Why it mattersThe newer, more capable category of tool — and the one that needs the most human oversight.

Algorithm

A set of steps a computer follows to solve a problem or make a decision.

Why it mattersEvery AI tool runs on an algorithm — it isn't a mysterious or exotic idea.

Predictive Analytics

Using past data, like sales history, to estimate what's likely to happen next, like next month's demand.

Why it mattersDirectly useful for inventory, staffing, and cash-flow planning.

Natural Language Processing (NLP)

The branch of AI focused on understanding and generating human language.

Why it mattersWhat lets a chatbot understand a customer's actual question, not just match keywords.

Computer Vision

AI that can interpret images or video — for example, scanning a receipt, reading a label, or checking shelf stock from a photo.

Why it mattersRelevant if your business handles a lot of physical inventory or paperwork.

API (Application Programming Interface)

A way for two pieces of software to talk to each other automatically.

Why it mattersIf a vendor says their AI tool "integrates via API" with your point-of-sale or accounting software, this is what they mean.

Cloud Software (SaaS)

Software you access online through a browser or app, rather than installing on one computer.

Why it mattersMost small-business AI tools work this way — no IT department required.

AI Hallucination

When an AI tool confidently states something that is inaccurate or made up.

Why it mattersThe single most important reason to have a person review AI output before it reaches a customer.

Human-in-the-Loop

A setup where a person reviews or approves an AI tool's output before it's finalized.

Why it mattersThe safest way for a small business to start using AI — with a human still checking the work.

Data Privacy

The practices that protect customer and business information from being misused or exposed when using digital tools, including AI.

Why it mattersKnow what a tool does with your customer data before you use it, not after.

Return on Investment (ROI)

The value or savings a tool produces, compared to what it costs you.

Why it mattersThe only real test of whether an AI tool is worth it for your specific business.
